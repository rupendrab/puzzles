Interactive Ball Coloring

The goal is to color the circles black and white so that no equilateral triangle in the figure has all three vertices the same color.

Click any ball to switch it between white and black.

Why This Has No Solution

Swapping every black circle with a white one does not change the problem, so we may assume without loss of generality that 0 = B.

The figure is symmetric under reflection, with the pairs 1 ↔ 2, 3 ↔ 5, 6 ↔ 9, and 7 ↔ 8. That leaves only two essentially different cases:

  • 1 = W, 2 = W
  • 1 = W, 2 = B

Case 1: 1 = W, 2 = W

From triangle (1, 2, 4), we get 4 = B.

If 3 = B, then triangle (1, 7, 5) forces either 7 = B or 5 = B. But that is impossible:

  • 3 = B already makes triangle (1, 3, 4) unable to use another black vertex.
  • 7 = B would make triangle (7, 3, 4) all black.
  • 5 = B would make triangle (0, 3, 5) all black.

So 3 ≠ B, hence 3 = W. Then triangle (2, 3, 8) gives 8 = B.

Now triangle (1, 5, 7) requires at least one of 5 or 7 to be black, since 1 = W. But both choices fail:

  • 5 = B makes triangle (4, 5, 8) all black.
  • 7 = B makes triangle (4, 7, 8) all black.

Therefore Case 1 is impossible.

Case 2: 1 = W, 2 = B

If 3 = B, then triangle (0, 3, 5) gives 5 = W, and triangle (1, 5, 7) gives 7 = B.

Now triangle (1, 6, 8) requires at least one of 6 or 8 to be black. But neither can be:

  • 6 = B makes triangle (3, 6, 7) all black.
  • 8 = B makes triangle (2, 3, 8) all black.

So 3 ≠ B, hence 3 = W.

Then the forced chain is: 4 = B from (1, 3, 4), 5 = W from (2, 4, 5), 7 = B from (1, 5, 7), 8 = W from (4, 7, 8), and 9 = B from (5, 8, 9).

But then triangle (2, 7, 9) is all black, a contradiction.

Therefore Case 2 is also impossible, so there is no valid coloring of the ten circles.
